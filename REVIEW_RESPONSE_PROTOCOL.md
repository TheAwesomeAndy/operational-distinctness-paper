# Review-Response Protocol (TAFFC)

A discipline for handling reviewer reports on the ARSPI-Net TAFFC submission. The goal is a
respectful, evidence-anchored response that maximizes the chance of acceptance without compromising
claim discipline or scientific integrity.

## When reviews arrive
- **Wait before reacting.** Read all reviews once, then set them aside before drafting anything. Do
  not respond from a first emotional reading.
- **Treat a revise-and-resubmit (major or minor) as a positive outcome.** It is an invitation to
  improve and resubmit, not a rejection.

## Drafting the response letter
- **Answer every comment, item by item.** Number each reviewer point and respond to it directly; do
  not bundle or skip points.
- **Cite exact locations for every change** — page, line, section, figure, or table number in the
  revised manuscript — so the editor and reviewer can verify each change quickly.
- **Treat misunderstandings as our clarity failures.** If a reviewer misread something, fix the text
  so the next reader cannot misread it, and say what was clarified — do not simply assert they were
  wrong.
- **Defend correct choices politely, with evidence.** Where a reviewer requests a change that would
  reduce correctness or overclaim, decline courteously and back the decision with the manuscript's
  evidence, scope, or data governance. Hold the claim-discipline line (no diagnostic validation, no
  online/real-time BCI, no classifier-superiority claim, no hardware-energy claim).
- **Never attack a reviewer** or speculate about their identity or motives in the letter.

## Integrity guards
- **No appeasement citations.** Do not add citations merely because a reviewer seems to want their
  own work cited; see `CITATION_INTEGRITY_POLICY.md`.
- **No fabricated results.** Any new experiment, metric, or baseline added in revision must be
  produced by a real script run and reported transparently, including any change to numbers.
- **Preserve scope sentences.** Keep the SHAPE-regime restriction and the not-claimed boundaries
  intact through every revision.

## Mechanics
- Maintain a change log mapping each reviewer point → manuscript change → location.
- Recompile both variants and re-run the validation checklist (page count, undefined refs/citations,
  multiply-defined labels, repeated bibitems, numeric-token preservation) before resubmitting.
- Keep the rebuttal letter focused and professional; brevity and precision read better than volume.
